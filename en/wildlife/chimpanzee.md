## 🐒 Common Chimpanzee - Humanity's Closest Relatives

### Overview

Chimpanzees are humanity's closest relatives, sharing 98.8% of our genes. They can manufacture and use tools, possess cultural transmission, and demonstrate remarkable intelligence and emotions. Jane Goodall's research at Gombe Stream changed human understanding of animal intelligence. Mahale Mountains and Gombe Stream are Tanzania's only chimpanzee habitats, also world's most famous chimpanzee research bases.

### Physical Characteristics

#### Body Features
- **Size**: Males 40-70kg, females 30-50kg
- **Height**: Males 120-170cm (standing), females 100-130cm
- **Arms**: Arms longer than legs, can reach knees when standing
- **Fur**: Black fur, face, palms, soles hairless

#### Unique Features
- **Face**: Exposed black face, face turns white with age
- **Ears**: Large round ears
- **Nose**: Flat nose
- **Thumb**: Opposable thumbs, capable of fine manipulation
- **Toes**: Toes can grasp like hands

### Behavior and Habits

#### Tool Use
- **Ant Fishing**: Using sticks to fish for ants, chimpanzees' most famous tool use behavior
- **Nut Cracking**: Using stones to crack nuts
- **Drinking Water**: Using leaves to drink water
- **Cleaning**: Using leaves to clean bodies

#### Social Structure
- **Group Living**: Chimpanzees form complex groups, size from 20-100 individuals
- **Male Alliances**: Males form alliances, defend territory together
- **Matrilineal Society**: Females usually stay in birth groups, males may migrate
- **Hierarchy**: Strict hierarchy within groups

#### Communication Methods
- **Vocalizations**: Use various calls for communication
- **Body Language**: Use gestures, facial expressions for communication
- **Hugging**: Hugging is important social behavior for chimpanzees
- **Kissing**: Kissing is how chimpanzees express friendliness

#### Reproduction and Offspring Rearing
- **Estrus**: Females can come into estrus year-round
- **Gestation**: About 8 months
- **Litter Size**: Usually one infant
- **Nursing Period**: About 4-5 years
- **Lifespan**: Wild 40-50 years, captivity up to 60 years

### Conservation Status

#### Threat Factors
- **Habitat Loss**: Deforestation reduces habitat
- **Poaching**: Poachers hunt chimpanzees for meat and body parts
- **Disease**: Human-transmitted diseases threaten chimpanzees
- **Climate Change**: Climate change affects habitat

#### Conservation Measures
- **Habitat Protection**: Establish national parks and protected areas
- **Anti-Poaching**: Combat poaching and illegal trade
- **Community Engagement**: Benefit local communities from conservation
- **Research Projects**: Continuously monitor chimpanzee populations and behavior

### Safari Observation Tips

#### Best Viewing Locations
1. **Mahale Mountains National Park**: Tanzania's largest chimpanzee population, about 800
2. **Gombe Stream National Park**: Jane Goodall's research base, about 100 chimpanzees

#### Best Viewing Times
- **Early Morning (6-9am)**: Chimpanzees most active, start feeding
- **Late Afternoon (4-7pm)**: Chimpanzees preparing to rest
- **Avoid**: Midday (chimpanzees rest in shade)

#### Observation Techniques
- **Stay Quiet**: Don't make loud noises
- **Keep Distance**: At least 7 meters, avoid disease transmission
- **Listen to Guides**: Guides know chimpanzee activity patterns best
- **Be Patient**: Chimpanzees may be in trees, need patience to wait
