## 🦓 Plains Zebra - The Black and White Knight of the Savanna

### Overview

The plains zebra is one of the most iconic animals on African savannas, famous for its unique black and white stripes. Each zebra's stripes are unique, like human fingerprints. The function of stripes is not fully determined, mainstream theories include: repelling flies, group identification, temperature regulation. Zebras are an important part of the Great Migration, with about 200,000 zebras migrating with 1.5 million wildebeest, forming Earth's most spectacular wildlife phenomenon.

### Physical Characteristics

#### Body Features
- **Size**: Males 175-385kg, females 175-320kg
- **Height**: Shoulder height 1.2-1.4 meters
- **Length**: 2-2.5 meters
- **Tail**: About 50cm long, with black hair tuft at end

#### Unique Features
- **Black and White Stripes**: Each zebra's stripes are unique, like human fingerprints
- **Mane**: Upright black mane, extending from head to back
- **Belly**: White belly, contrasting with back stripes
- **Hooves**: Hard hooves, suitable for running on savannas

#### Stripe Features
- **Head**: Narrower stripes, concentrated on face
- **Neck**: Wider stripes, forming distinct patterns
- **Body**: Wider stripes in middle of body, gradually narrowing toward tail
- **Legs**: Narrower stripes on legs, forming vertical stripes

### Behavior and Habits

#### Great Migration

**Migration Route**:
- **Southern Serengeti**: December-March, zebras breed in south
- **Western Serengeti**: April-June, zebras migrate west
- **Northern Serengeti**: July-October, zebras migrate north, crossing Mara River
- **Masai Mara**: October-November, zebras stay in Masai Mara, then return to Serengeti

**Migration Scale**:
- About 200,000 zebras
- Migrate with 1.5 million wildebeest, 350,000 Thomson's gazelles

#### Feeding Behavior

**Food Selection**:
- **Primary Food**: Various grasses (90%+ of diet)
- **Secondary**: Occasionally eat leaves and twigs
- **Daily Requirement**: Adult zebras need 5-7kg of grass daily

**Feeding Methods**:
- Mainly feed at dawn and dusk
- Rest in shade during day, ruminate food
- Often travel long distances seeking food and water
- Can survive for weeks without water in drought conditions

#### Social Structure
- **Group Living**: Zebras are extremely social, groups range from few to hundreds
- **Group Composition**: Usually led by one male zebra, containing multiple females and foals
- **Bachelor Groups**: Unpaired male zebras form bachelor groups
- **Social Behavior**: Mutual grooming, strengthen group cohesion

#### Defense and Attack
- **Kick Power**: Zebra kick power is extremely strong, can crush lion skulls
- **Running Speed**: Top speed 65km/h, can flee quickly
- **Group Defense**: When facing predators, flee in groups, forming spectacular scenes
- **Warning**: Zebras make loud calls, warning group members

### Reproduction and Foal Rearing

#### Reproductive Cycle
- **Estrus**: Females can come into estrus year-round, but peak during rainy season
- **Gestation**: About 12-13 months
- **Litter Size**: Usually one foal, twins extremely rare

#### Foal Development
- **Birth**: Weight 30-35kg, can stand immediately
- **Rapid Growth**: Foals can run within hours of birth
- **Follow Mother**: Foals immediately follow mother, avoiding predation
- **Sexual Maturity**: Females 2-3 years, males 3-4 years
- **Birth Interval**: Usually one foal per year

**Foal Survival Challenges**:
- Foal mortality rate about 50%, mainly from predation (lions, leopards, hyenas, crocodiles)
- Mother zebras protect foals, using kicks against predators
- Foals must learn to run within hours, otherwise get preyed upon

### Conservation Status

#### History and Current Status
- **Historical Population**: About 750,000 in early 20th century
- **Current Population**: About 500,000
- **Tanzania**: About 150,000, mainly distributed in Serengeti

#### Major Threats
1. **Habitat Loss**:
   - Agricultural expansion encroaches on habitat
   - Human settlements fragment habitat

2. **Human-Wildlife Conflict**:
   - Zebras damage crops
   - Competition with livestock for food and water

3. **Drought**:
   - Climate change leads to intensified drought
   - Reduced water sources affect survival

4. **Poaching**:
   - Zebra skins used for decorations
   - Zebra meat used as food

#### Conservation Measures
- **Habitat Protection**: Establish national parks and protected areas
- **Migration Corridor Protection**: Protect migration routes, avoid barriers
- **Community Engagement**: Benefit local communities from conservation
- **Monitoring Projects**: Monitor zebra populations and distribution

### Safari Observation Tips

#### Best Viewing Locations
1. **Serengeti**: East Africa's largest zebra population, about 150,000
2. **Ngorongoro**: Bottom of crater, often see zebra herds
3. **Tarangire**: Numerous zebras, easy to observe
4. **Mara River**: July-October, can see zebras crossing Mara River

#### Best Viewing Times
- **Early Morning (6-9am)**: Zebras most active, heading to feed
- **Late Afternoon (4-7pm)**: Zebras returning from forest to savanna
- **Avoid**: Midday (zebras rest in shade)

#### Behavior Identification
- **Group Movement**: Moving in groups, forming spectacular scenes
- **Kicking**: Zebras kick predators with hind legs
- **Grooming**: Zebras groom each other, strengthen group cohesion
- **Foals**: Seeing newborn foals running immediately

#### Photography Tips
- **Lens**: 100-400mm, wide angle for spectacular scenes of zebra herds
- **Composition**: Capture scale of zebra herds and contrast of stripes
- **Behavior**: Photograph zebras crossing rivers, running behaviors
- **Lighting**: Side light in early morning and evening works best

#### Observation Techniques
- **Look for Migration Routes**: Zebras usually move along fixed migration routes
- **Watch Predators**: Lions, leopards, hyenas often track zebra herds
- **Listen to Guides**: Guides know zebra activity patterns best
- **Keep Distance**: Don't interfere with zebra feeding

### Interesting Facts

1. **Unique Stripes**: Each zebra's stripes are unique, like human fingerprints
2. **Stripe Function**: Zebra stripe functions include repelling flies, group identification, temperature regulation
3. **Kick Power**: Zebra kick power is extremely strong, can crush lion skulls
4. **Sleep**: Zebras usually sleep standing, ready to flee at any time
5. **Running Speed**: Zebras can run up to 65km/h
6. **Foal Recognition**: Foals recognize mothers by stripe patterns

## 🌍 Ecological Significance

Zebras are important members of African ecosystems, maintaining savanna balance by consuming large amounts of grass. Zebras are also important prey for many predators (like lions, leopards, hyenas, crocodiles), key links in the food chain. The Great Migration of zebras is one of Earth's most spectacular wildlife phenomena, iconic landscape of African savannas. Protecting zebras is not just protecting a species, but preserving ecological integrity and biodiversity of entire ecosystems.
